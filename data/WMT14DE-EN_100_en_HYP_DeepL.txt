Schools are being urged to place greater emphasis on mathematics, spelling and grammar
In English literature lessons, pupils will in future be required to study at least one play by Shakespeare, a 19th-century novel, Romantic poetry and contemporary British novels from 1914 onwards.
‘Unseen texts’ will also be included in the exam to encourage a wider range of reading.
The combined English Literature and Language course is to be abolished.
From 2015, pupils will have to sit a separate GCSE exam in Language, although there will be strong incentives to choose English Literature as a separate qualification.
The Department for Education will publish the new syllabuses for English and Maths tomorrow – the first subjects to undergo a radical overhaul.
Other core subjects will be revised next year.
In a separate initiative, Ofqual, the exam regulator, will announce a reorganisation of the GCSE structure, including a new marking scheme and less coursework.
In a speech this summer, Education Secretary Michael Gove said there was a “broad consensus that we need to reform our examination system to restore public confidence”, emphasising that GCSEs would become “more challenging, more ambitious and more rigorous”.
Studies show that English schools devote less time to maths – 116 hours per year, or three hours a week over the school year – than most other countries.
By contrast, Australian schools teach an average of 143 hours a year, and pupils in Singapore receive 138 hours.
Whilst there will be no formal requirement to allocate more time to maths in the timetable, sources within the coalition suggest that the comprehensive maths exam – combined with the subject being given greater weighting in league tables – is likely to encourage schools to schedule more hours.
The curriculum will place greater emphasis on ‘real-life problems’, including financial mathematics.
Rapist in South Africa: two life sentences
Nine months after the brutal rape and mutilation of a 17-year-old girl in South Africa, the perpetrator was sentenced on Friday to two life sentences.
This was reported by the SABC.
The victim later died as a result of her serious injuries.
Johannes Kana had already been found guilty earlier in the week.
The crime had sparked nationwide horror.
The then 21-year-old had raped Anene Booysen in February at an industrial estate in Bredasdorp, near Cape Town.
Doctors fought for 14 hours to save the victim’s life, but to no avail.
The sight of the young woman was so horrific that the carers and nurses required psychological support.
During the trial, Kana had admitted to having been in a bar with Booysen on the night of the crime.
He also confessed to having beaten and raped her, but denied being responsible for the girl’s death.
The police had initially assumed there were several perpetrators, but released two suspects from custody because the evidence against them was insufficient.
The prosecution also emphasised during the trial that it was convinced Kana had been alone at the time of the attack.
Rape is almost an everyday occurrence in South Africa: there are around 64,000 reports of sexual violence each year.
That is almost ten times as many as in Germany, which, however, with a population of 82 million, has significantly more inhabitants than South Africa (50 million).
The police and women’s organisations in South Africa estimate, however, that the number of unreported cases of sexual violence is 10 to 25 times higher.

The stock exchange operator Nasdaq OMX continues to keep its clients in suspense.
Trading on the Nasdaq Options Market was suspended on Friday afternoon, German time.
In a statement, the operator blamed technical problems.
The other eleven US options markets, including two operated by Nasdaq OMX, continued trading without disruption.
This latest incident continues a whole series of minor and major glitches at the exchange operator.
As recently as Tuesday, the Nasdaq indices had not been calculated for an hour due to errors in data transmission.
In August this year, there were two glitches in the space of a single week.
First, the US investment bank Goldman Sachs had sent a massive number of faulty buy orders to the options markets due to technical problems.
As a result, the exchange operators took almost a whole day to review and cancel the orders.
Two days later, half of all share trading in the US ground to a halt for several hours due to a computer glitch at the Nasdaq stock exchange.
Facebook’s initial public offering also caused a stir last year.
The Nasdaq systems had been unable to cope with the flood of buy and sell orders, the US Securities and Exchange Commission (SEC) later found, and it imposed a record fine of 10 million dollars on the company.
Whistleblower Edward Snowden is, in principle, willing to hold talks with the German authorities.
However, he first wants his situation to be clarified.
The former NSA employee, who is wanted by the US, made this clear in an unaddressed letter which Green Party politician Hans-Christian Ströbele published on Friday following a meeting with Snowden, and which he forwarded to the Federal Government, the Bundestag and the Federal Public Prosecutor General.
According to a translation circulated by Ströbele, the letter reads as follows:
To the relevant authorities
I have been asked to write to you regarding your investigation into mass surveillance.
My name is Edward Joseph Snowden and I was previously employed, either on a contract basis or as a direct employee, as a technical expert at the National Security Agency (NSA), the Central Intelligence Agency (CIA) and the Defence Intelligence Agency (DIA) of the United States.
In the course of my employment at these agencies, I witnessed systematic violations of the law by my government, which compelled me to act out of a moral duty.
As a result of publicising these concerns, I found myself subjected to severe and sustained persecution, which forced me to leave my family and my homeland.
I am currently living in exile and am granted temporary asylum by the Russian Federation in accordance with international law.
I am encouraged by the response to my political actions, both in the US and elsewhere.
Citizens around the world, as well as senior officials – including those in the United States – have regarded the revelations about a system of all-encompassing surveillance that is accountable to no one as a service to the public.
These revelations about espionage have led to numerous proposals for new laws and policies aimed at addressing the previously covert abuse of public trust.
The benefits to society of these insights are becoming increasingly clear; at the same time, the risks involved have been visibly reduced.
Although the outcome of my efforts has demonstrably been positive, my government continues to treat dissent as a breach of loyalty and seeks to criminalise and prosecute the expression of political opinion.
Nevertheless, speaking the truth is not a crime.
I am confident that the Government of the United States, with the support of the international community, will abandon this detrimental stance.
I hope that, once the difficulties of this humanitarian situation have been resolved, I will be in a position to participate, in an appropriate and lawful manner, in the responsible clarification of the facts regarding the statements made in the media, particularly with regard to the truth and authenticity of the reports.
I look forward to speaking with you in your country once the situation has been clarified, and I thank you for your efforts to uphold international law, which protects us all.

Driver who sped at 210 km/h with a hot drink between his legs receives a £1,000 fine
A motorist has been fined £1,000 for driving at speeds of up to 210 km/h with a hot drink between his legs.
Andrew Howie, 35, from Tiptree, Essex, was stopped in his Mercedes-Benz on the A120 near Braintree on 27 May.
After the police had stopped him, they discovered the drink cup between his legs.
At Colchester Magistrates’ Court, Howie pleaded guilty to driving without due care and attention.
He was given seven penalty points and a six-month driving ban.
Howie was also ordered to pay costs of £90 and a victim surcharge of £100.
Tragedy in Uruguay: Boys kill playmate with machete
Two children in Uruguay have confessed to the murder of an 11-year-old boy.
The two boys, aged 14 and 12, had invited their victim to go bird hunting with them.
Armed with machetes and knives, they stabbed the 11-year-old to death on Tuesday and threw the body into a well, the newspaper “El País” reported on Thursday.
They then reportedly played football outside their homes in a working-class neighbourhood.
The body was found the following night in the village of Cerro Pelado, 15 kilometres north of the exclusive seaside resort of Punta del Este.
As an alibi, a five-year-old half-sister of the younger boy, whom they had taken with them, was to testify that she had been sexually assaulted by the boy.
However, when the girl described the sequence of events to the police without mentioning any assault against her, the two boys admitted to the premeditated killing of the 11-year-old.
Among other things, the older boy cited a rivalry during a football match as the reason.
He has been provisionally admitted to a psychiatric youth facility.
Government in Jerusalem does not confirm attack on Syrian air base
An American government official, who wished to remain anonymous, told the American news channel CNN on Thursday that Israeli fighter jets had attacked an airbase in Latakia, Syria, the previous day.
According to CNN, the targets were “missiles and associated equipment” which the Israeli government feared were intended for the Lebanese Hezbollah.
Syrian and Lebanese media, as well as the Arab broadcaster Al-Arabiya, had previously reported that the base in the port city of Latakia had been shelled during the night from Wednesday to Thursday, and had blamed the Israelis for the attack.
The Israeli television channel Channel 2 showed satellite images of the base in Latakia, which revealed Russian S-125 Neva air defence missiles and a battery of SA-3 missiles, which, according to Channel 2, have a range of 35 kilometres and can carry warheads weighing up to 70 kilograms.
Uncertain The government in Jerusalem has not officially confirmed the attack.
However, the Reuters news agency quotes an Israeli government official, who also spoke on condition of anonymity, as saying that he believed Israel had carried out the attack, but was not certain.
A spokesperson for the Israeli Ministry of Defence said, in any case: “We do not comment on these reports”.
Journalist Ron Ben-Yishai emphasised in the daily newspaper Yedioth Ahronoth that the Syrian regime had previously attempted, in some cases successfully, to supply surface-to-air missiles to Hezbollah.
The Israeli government had repeatedly warned that any attempt by Syria to supply Hezbollah with chemical or other dangerous weapons would constitute crossing a “red line”, which would be met with a military response.
